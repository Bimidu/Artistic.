Literature Survey

Background & Related Work

Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) is a neurodevelopmental condition characterized by differences in social communication and interaction. Clinical diagnosis relies on expert assessment and structured instruments, which are time-intensive and not always accessible in low-resource settings.

Computational ASD screening from speech has explored acoustic, lexical-syntactic, and pragmatic discourse markers. The TalkBank ecosystem provides standardized CHAT transcripts and open corpora, including ASD-related studies from Eigsti et al. (2007) and Rollins (1999), which highlight pragmatic and linguistic differences in autism. Prior research also shows that conversational timing and semantic patterns in speech can reveal ASD-related communication characteristics.

Research Gap

What Was Missing

Many research prototypes remain disconnected from deployable systems due to three key limitations:

Limited Input Handling

Existing systems handle only one modality, missing the complementary signals available in real clinical settings where both transcripts and audio exist.

Insufficient Preprocessing Rigor

Prior pipelines lack leakage-safe splitting, robust imputation, and speaker-diarization handling, leading to overfit models that fail in deployment with noisy or short sessions.

Absence of Interpretability

No prior system provides both global feature attributions and instance-level counterfactual explanations, making it impossible for clinicians to audit or trust predictions.

Research Problem

Problem Statement

How can a multimodal computational system reliably screen for autism spectrum disorder from child-caregiver interactions, accepting both audio recordings and CHAT-format transcripts, while providing transparent, clinician-facing explanations that link model outputs to observable conversational behaviors?

This work focuses on screening rather than diagnosis, providing an early, supportive tool that can assist, but not replace, clinical evaluation. Speech and conversation provide ecologically valid behavioral signals: pragmatic competence, conversational coordination, response relevance, and prosodic regulation.

Research Objectives

What We Set Out to Achieve

1
Build a unified multimodal input pipeline

Accept CHAT files and audio (WAV/MP3/FLAC) through a common interface, with speaker diarization and child-segment isolation for acoustic analysis.

2
Extract 400+ features across three modalities

Implement composable, auditable feature modules for pragmatic-conversational (207), acoustic-prosodic (153), and syntactic-semantic (27) signals, each with explicit feature lists for reproducibility.

3
Train component-specific models and a fusion layer

SVM+RFECV for pragmatic, Random Forest for acoustic, Gradient Boosting for syntactic. Combine via weighted fusion (50% / 25% / 25%).

4
Deliver SHAP and counterfactual explanations

Global feature importance summaries and instance-level waterfall plots via SHAP. Neural-autoencoder-based counterfactuals showing minimal behavioral changes that would shift predictions.

5
Deploy as a reproducible, scalable platform

FastAPI backend with a React/Next.js frontend, persisting intermediate transcriptions, feature CSVs, and model metadata for reproducible evaluation.

Methodology

Five-Layer System Architecture

A modular Python pipeline exposed via a FastAPI backend, spanning input handling through to explanation generation.

1

Input Handling & Transcription

Accepts CHAT .cha files (parsed via CHATParser) or audio. Audio is transcribed by AssemblyAI with speaker diarization; child segments are isolated by ChildAudioExtractor. Implemented in src/pipeline/input_handler.py.

2

Preprocessing

Leakage-safe stratified train/test split. Cleaning: infinite-value replacement, median imputation, outlier clipping. Acoustic features additionally undergo variance thresholding. Implemented in src/preprocessing/.

3

Feature Extraction

A central FeatureExtractor orchestrates three parallel modules, pragmatic (207), acoustic (153), syntactic (27), merging outputs. Each module exposes explicit feature lists for consistent ordering and scaling. Implemented in src/features/.

4

Modeling & Fusion

SVM+RFECV (pragmatic), Random Forest (acoustic), Gradient Boosting (syntactic). Weighted fusion 50%/25%/25%. Strategies also include voting, averaging, and stacking. Implemented in src/models/ and src/pipeline/model_fusion.py.

5

Explanation & Visualization

SHAP global summary plots and local waterfall plots. Neural autoencoder generates counterfactuals with validity 1.00 and normalized L2 distance 0.288. Annotated transcripts mark [TURN], [PAUSE], [REPAIR], [TOPIC]. Implemented in src/interpretability/.

Results

Experimental Results & Explainability

Key Findings

Multimodal fusion achieves 87.2% accuracy and 0.92 ROC-AUC, outperforming unimodal baselines by 2, 4%. The pragmatic component provides the strongest predictive signal, with RFECV reducing 214 features to 30 most discriminative.

Selected features emphasize interactional timing: long pause ratios, child initiation ratios, semantic coherence variability, topic drift, lexical overlap, filled pause density, and repair success indicators.

Limitations

Dataset heterogeneity across ASDBank corpora and limited sample sizes constrain generalization. The age range (2, 7 years) introduces developmental variability not explicitly modeled. The syntactic component underperforms at 74.2% due to weaker discriminative signal in syntactic structure.

Future work: Cross-corpus validation on diverse TalkBank datasets, calibrated fusion with uncertainty estimation, sparsity-constrained counterfactual generation, and clinical trials. IRB approval pending for clinical pilots with adherence to IEEE P2801 fairness standards.
